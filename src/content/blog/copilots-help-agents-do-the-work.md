A **copilot** sits next to a person and makes them faster. It drafts the email, suggests the code, summarises the ticket. The person still does the job: they decide, they click, they own the result.

An **agent** takes the job on. It reads the ticket, checks the records, applies the policy and prepares the finished change: the refund, the bill, the pull request.

## The model is no longer the bottleneck

Today's models can already do much of this work. What stops most companies from letting them isn't capability. It's:

- not knowing what the agent **will** do,
- not being able to **stop** it,
- and not being able to **prove** afterwards what it did.

## What it takes to hand work over

| Requirement | What it means |
|---|---|
| **A person's yes** | A decision *before* the change, not a log to read afterwards |
| **Proof before release** | A test suite of real cases every new version has to pass |
| **A record of every step** | What started the work, what the agent did, who approved it, what it cost |
| **Least access** | Each agent can reach only what its job needs |

## Where that leaves people

> With the decisions, not the legwork.

The agent does the research and prepares the change; a person signs off in one step instead of doing the work or redoing it. That's the line we build to, in every product.
